# MaxGPT-Ultra: Deep Research Sweep, Final Report
**Date: 22 Sep 2026. Scope: 1.1B dense decoder, 100B tokens, 4x TITAN RTX (Turing, fp16), pretraining a few hours in, decay phase and all post-training not yet started.**

---

## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. **The recipe is still state of the art for this scale.** NorMuon + cautious WD, per-head sigmoid attention-output gate, normalized value residual, QK-norm, GQA, RoPE, RMSNorm, tied embeddings, WSD: nothing published between now and Sep 2026 beats this combination at 1.1B dense with evidence strong enough to act on.
2. **Nothing clears the restart bar.** The bar: a clear multi-percent held-out-loss win, or a large speed win at zero quality cost. The single best-evidenced architecture change of 2026 (Softpick, +0.009 CLIMB-avg at 1.2B, z = +4.47) is worth about as much as one free post-hoc checkpoint average that costs nothing, and its only fast kernel is FlashAttention-2, which sm_75 cannot run.
3. **Everything genuinely worth doing lives in the decay phase, the data pipeline, or post-training**, all of which are still ahead of you. That is the useful result of this sweep, not a list of things to change today.
4. **The Turing hardware costs you speed and options, not quality.** Details in section 1a. The one place where fp16 could have cost quality (the Newton-Schulz path) was audited against the actual code and is structurally safe here.
5. **Biggest single lever remaining: decay-phase data annealing.** Prepare the shards now, while the stable phase runs, because the decay leg arrives on a fixed wall-clock schedule and there is no second chance at it.

## 1a. Your four questions, answered

**"Do the older architectures change model quality, or just the speed at which it trains?"**

Just the speed. TITAN RTX is an older *GPU generation*, not an older *model architecture*. The model, the optimizer, the data and the schedule are identical objects to what would run on the 5070. Two specific differences, both speed-only:

- **No bf16, so you run fp16 autocast + dynamic loss scaling instead.** This is the pre-2020 industry-standard mixed-precision recipe and it was shown to match fp32 accuracy. Master weights, optimizer state and accumulation all stay fp32; only the matmul inputs are 16-bit. fp16 actually has *more* mantissa precision than bf16 (10 bits vs 7); what it lacks is exponent range (max 65504), which is exactly the thing loss scaling exists to fix, and scaling by powers of two is exact, so it introduces no rounding error of its own.
- **No FlashAttention-2, so you use PyTorch SDPA's memory-efficient backend.** FA2 is an IO-aware rewrite of the same softmax attention, not a different function, and the mem-efficient backend still accumulates in fp32.

The honest asterisk: fp16 is *twitchier* than bf16, not less accurate. It raises the odds of an overflow-driven divergence you have to roll back from. That is a schedule risk, not a quality ceiling, and the trainer already has a rollback guard for it. A scout spent a full investigation on the one published way fp16 + Newton-Schulz can silently poison Muon momentum (DeepSpeed issues #8432 / #8533) and found it **cannot fire in this trainer**: `torch.amp.GradScaler._maybe_opt_step` skips `optimizer.step()` entirely on an overflow step, so the momentum buffer is never touched, and `scaler.unscale_` plus `clip_grad_norm_(1.0)` run before Muon ever sees a gradient, so the Frobenius norm going into Newton-Schulz is bounded by 1 and sits about four orders of magnitude below the fp16 ceiling.

**"Was the 100B built the exact same as the one at home, or new random tokens?"**

Same pipeline, same mixture spec, same seed, but a **fresh re-stream on the Lambda box**, not a byte-copy of the home shards. `data/prepare.py` defines one `PRETRAIN_MIX` and `MixedStream` samples it with `random.Random(seed)` defaulting to `seed=0`, so the document interleaving is deterministic and reproducible. `configs/ultra_lambda.yaml` extends `configs/ultra.yaml` specifically so the architecture object and the checkpoints stay interchangeable with the home box. It is not "new random tokens" in any bad sense: same corpus, same blend, re-drawn. The one thing nobody verified is whether the upstream HuggingFace dataset revisions moved since the home build. If they did, you get different documents at the same seed, which is quality-neutral.

**"What was the split again?"**

Realized **token** shares, which is what matters: roughly **55% FineWeb-Edu-dedup, 22% Cosmopedia-v2, 10% CodeParrot code, 8% OpenWebMath, 5% Wikipedia**. The task brief this sweep was given said 55/23/11/8/3, which is close but has Wikipedia low.

Important reading note, because one scout got this backwards and it changes a recommendation: the numbers in `data/prepare.py` (0.55 / 0.32 / 0.037 / 0.039 / 0.048) are **document sampling probabilities, not token shares**. They were deliberately detuned from a measured shakedown run because code documents over-count about 2.3x and Cosmopedia snippets under-count about 0.6x by length. Raw doc-weights of 0.55/0.22/0.10/0.08/0.05 produced token shares of 46/13/23/14/4, which is why the weights look strange. So you do **not** have a 32%-synthetic, 4%-code model. Read the actual realized numbers off `data/shards/meta.json` on the Lambda box (it is not in the Mac checkout) before you size the anneal mix.

**"Same quality, just faster on the Titans rather than the 5070?"**

Yes on quality, with the fp16 caveat above (same converged quality, higher chance of a divergence you restart from a checkpoint). On speed, be precise about *why* it is faster: a single TITAN RTX is not faster than a 5070. The Titans win because there are four of them now and six to eight once IT sets the power cap. Per card, Turing is the slower silicon, and it also costs you fp8, bf16 and FA2 as options.

## 2. Apply in flight (no restart)

Ordered by value. Items 1 and 2 are the only ones with a deadline attached.

### 2.1 Confirm the fp16 path is healthy (5 minutes, do it today)

**Expected gain:** none. This closes a question permanently so it does not get re-litigated.

On the box, from `~/MaxGPT/maxgpt-ultra`:

```
grep -o '"loss_scale": [0-9.e+]*' runs/ultra/metrics.jsonl | tail -60
grep -c '"event": "divergence"' runs/ultra/metrics.jsonl
grep -o '"loss": [0-9.]*' runs/ultra/metrics.jsonl | tail -20
```

Healthy loss scale is 2^14 to 2^17, occasionally halving and climbing back. Pinned at 128, or monotonically falling with no recovery, is the failure. Divergence count zero means no NaN ever reached the weights. The logged loss is the true unscaled cross-entropy (`_micro_forward` detaches before `scaler.scale()`), so a falling curve there is real evidence.

**When:** now.

### 2.2 Stop the checkpoint pruner deleting your options (do at the next restart, before decay)

**Expected gain:** none directly. It is the precondition for 2.5 and for any end-of-run A/B, and without it that option is gone.

`configs/ultra.yaml:45` sets `io.keep_last_k: 3`, `train/checkpoint.py:65` deletes everything older, and `train/trainer.py:140` reads `keep_last` once at `CheckpointManager` construction. At the end of the run you will have exactly three checkpoints and no averaging or comparison is possible even in principle.

Steps: add a weights-only side-save through the decay window (roughly every 2000 steps, which is 1B tokens) with its own retention of 10 to 12, and leave the full optimizer-state checkpoints at `keep_last_k: 3`. Disk cost matters: a full checkpoint at 1.1B includes optimizer state and is roughly 13 to 18 GB, so keeping 12 of those is about 200 GB on a shared box. Weights-only fp32 is about 4.4 GB each, so 12 is about 53 GB, which is fine. Check the scratch quota first, you have no root.

**When:** this takes effect at the next process restart, which you are already expecting when IT sets the power cap and you go to 6 or 8 cards. Do it then. Do not take a dedicated restart.

### 2.3 Decay-phase data annealing (prepare now, apply at the decay)

**Expected gain:** the largest lever still available. Honest band at 1.1B / 100B: **+2 to +5 points of benchmark average**, concentrated on math, knowledge and instruction-formatted tasks. Held-out loss will look *worse* during the anneal because the distribution changed. Do not judge this one on loss.

The evidence is unusually clean because MiniCPM ablated exactly this (same model, same decay length, only the decay data changed). Their 2.4B run, pretrain-only decay vs pretrain + high-quality/SFT decay: C-Eval 40.0 to 52.6, MMLU 44.6 to 50.9, GSM8K 27.7 to 42.3, HumanEval 27.7 to 30.4. Their 1.2B run shows the same pattern with one regression (GSM8K 34.2 to 31.8). Critically, their B-2 arm shows that spending the same 12B tokens as plain SFT *after* a pretrain-only decay gets almost nothing (C-Eval 40.9 to 41.2). The win comes from putting the data inside the decay. OLMo 2's Dolmino anneal gives +9.9 average at 7B on the same principle.

Steps:
1. Decide the mix now. Published guard rail (Zamba2, MiniCPM, OLMo 3): **roughly 60% replay of the original pretraining mix, 40% annealing data**, at most one epoch over most new sources and two over the best ones.
2. Candidate free sources, all on the Hub: FineMath-4+ and InfiWebMath-3+ (or MegaMath), Stack-edu, an upweighted OpenWebMath, a chat-formatted slice of UltraChat/OASST, and optionally PleIAs SYNTH. Keep synthetic reasoning at or below about 40% of the anneal half and keep a held-out non-synthetic loss track so you can tell real improvement from eval-flavored improvement.
3. Tokenize with the existing 49,152 byte-level BPE into the same shard format. This is CPU work on the login node and does not touch the GPUs.
4. Add a second mixture config plus a switch at the decay step in the data loader. No architecture change, no optimizer change, no restart.
5. Ramp the mix over roughly 1B tokens rather than switching in one step, to avoid a visible loss discontinuity.
6. Make sure the new shards are disjoint from what has already been consumed, or the "100B unique tokens" claim quietly stops being true.

Note that because the realized code and math shares are about 10% and 8% (not the 23%/14% a naive doc-weight reading would suggest, and not the sub-4% a different misreading suggests), this is a normal-sized anneal, not a rescue operation. Do it, but do not overweight math to the point of regressing general text.

**When:** data prep starts now. The switch fires at the start of the decay phase. Data prep is on the critical path: if the shards are not ready when the decay arrives you will either stall the run or default to the old mix.

### 2.4 Expand the eval harness before the decay

**Expected gain:** no direct quality gain. It is the instrument that makes 2.3 and everything in section 3 measurable instead of guessed.

`eval/harness.py` currently runs HellaSwag and ARC-Easy at n=200 each. That is 2 of the 12 CLIMB tasks at a sample size where per-task noise exceeds most of the effects you are trying to detect. It matters more than usual here: a controlled 20-method study at 1.2B and 3B found the loss-to-downstream decoupling is **concentrated in attention-output modifications**, which is exactly the family your per-head sigmoid gate belongs to, and two such modifications landed within 2-3% of baseline validation loss while dropping 6 to 16 points of downstream average.

Steps: add LAMBADA (the specific single-key-lookup probe for attention-output damage), PIQA, WinoGrande and ARC-Challenge; raise n substantially; or just swap in `lm-evaluation-harness`. Record a same-checkpoint repeat to establish your own noise floor. Fix one suite and one seed policy and run it at three points (end of pretraining, post-SFT, post-DPO) plus the continuous cheap held-out-loss probe.

**When:** before the decay starts.

### 2.5 Post-hoc checkpoint averaging / Tail-EMA (decide at the end, costs nothing)

**Expected gain:** small and contested. Keep expectations at roughly **+0.5 to +1.5% average downstream, possibly zero**. The single closest-scale measurement is negative: at 1.5B params / 30B tokens with uniformly shuffled data (your exact data ordering), EMA scored 45.29 core average vs 46.21 for a plain WSD decay. IMU-1, the paper your value-residual + NorMuon + cautious-WD recipe came from, measured +0.014 average at 430M with EMA beta 0.8 over the last 10 checkpoints. Both are cheap to test.

Steps: keep the last 10 to 12 weights-only checkpoints from 2.2, average the **fp32 master weights** on CPU (never the fp16 copies, never the optimizer state), evaluate the average against the final iterate on held-out loss plus the full suite, and ship whichever wins. Run the averaging window entirely inside the decay phase or entirely inside the stable phase, never straddling the data-mix switch from 2.3.

**Do not** replace the decay with a merge. WSM's headline (+2.04% average) is merge-*instead-of*-decay at 16.3B MoE on 10.2T tokens with a 100B-token merge window, and the paper explicitly reports that both "Decay-then-Merge" and "Merge-then-Decay" "failed to yield any improvement". Keep the decay.

**When:** at the end. It is post-hoc, so the worst case is you throw the average away.

### 2.6 Decay shape and length (one config line, decide before the decay)

**Expected gain:** 0.005 to 0.015 nats, mostly from shape. Small.

`train/schedule.py` already decays cosine to true zero (`min_lr_ratio` defaults to 0.0 and nothing overrides it), which is correct: the "Straight to Zero" 60%-compute-saving headline is a win over decaying to 10% of peak, which you are not doing. So there is nothing urgent here.

Two optional changes, both one line, both must be made **before** the decay begins: lengthen 15% to 20% (IMU-1 measured 20% matching a full cosine, 10% badly undertrained, 30% worse), and swap cosine for 1-sqrt (IMU-1 uses it, WSM derives it independently). At a fixed 100B budget, going to 20% shortens the stable phase by 5B tokens.

**Do not** raise the terminal LR to about 1/3 of peak on the strength of arXiv 2511.18903. That recommendation comes bundled with replacing the decay with averaging, it is one paper at 1.5B / 30B (a 20:1 token ratio against your 90:1), and taking half of it gets you the worst of both.

### 2.7 fp32 softmax and RMSNorm, plus activation telemetry (cheap insurance)

**Expected gain:** no loss improvement. It converts a class of run-ending fp16 overflow into a non-event and, more usefully, tells you whether you have headroom at all.

The only published technical report of a ~2B dense model actually pretrained in fp16 on pre-Ampere hardware (PCMind-2.1-Kaiyuan-2B, Ascend 910A) states its policy as fp32 for Softmax and RMSNorm, fp16 for everything else, and reports max activations exceeding 10,000 after 1T tokens against the fp16 ceiling of 65504. A separate controlled study at 7B measures the peak activation spike at 3,818 for a plain pre-norm SwiGLU baseline and **92 with sandwich norm + QK-norm, 186 with a conditional per-head attention gate**, at essentially unchanged perplexity. You already have QK-norm and the per-head gate, which is precisely the pair that suppresses the dangerous magnitudes. So the expectation is that you have roughly 300x headroom.

Steps: force fp32 for softmax and every RMSNorm in the autocast policy; add two scalars per eval to `metrics.jsonl`, max absolute pre-softmax attention logit and max absolute post-SwiGLU activation, per layer. Cost 1 to 3% step time.

The unverified interaction worth measuring rather than assuming: none of those studies had a normalized value residual injecting layer-1 values into every layer, which is an extra accumulation path into the residual stream and the most plausible remaining source of magnitude growth in this specific model.

**When:** any time, no restart needed for the telemetry; the autocast policy change takes effect at the next restart.

### 2.8 Throughput items worth a measurement, not a promise

- **flash-attention-turing** (`ssiu/flash-attention-turing`). Real FA2 kernels for sm_75 with a working backward, head dim 128, causal, GQA, fp16. Benchmarked on T4 at up to 2.19x forward and 1.35-1.51x backward against PyTorch SDPA. This would be a 10-20% end-to-end win with zero quality change. **The blocker is real**: the repo targets CUDA 12.4 with torch 2.5.1 or 2.8.0, and you are on cu118 with driver 470 and no root. Check whether it builds at all before spending time on it. If it does, validate before adopting: compare output and dQ/dK/dV against `F.scaled_dot_product_attention` in fp32 at your exact shapes, then run a few hundred steps from the current checkpoint on both paths and confirm the loss curves overlap.
- **DDP `fp16_compress_hook` and `gradient_as_bucket_view`**, and making sure `torch.compile` wraps DDP rather than the reverse. These are free, version-independent, and reported at 1.4-2.1x on the allreduce and +10-23% MFU respectively. On a box with no NVLink the comms win is worth having. Watch grad norms after enabling fp16 gradient compression, since that is a second place for underflow to bite on top of fp16 autocast.
- **Fused linear cross-entropy** (Liger `FusedLinearCrossEntropy` or Apple's Cut Cross-Entropy). Could buy `micro_batch: 2`, which `scripts/bench_micro.py` measured at 7,997 vs 7,385 tok/s on one Titan (+8.3%). Discount it: `loss_chunk: 2048` already does a cruder version of the same thing, so the marginal gain is smaller than the headline. If you try it, the risk is tied embeddings, exactly the case least likely to be tested upstream. Validate the fused loss and gradient against the current chunked path to fp16 tolerance on real batches first.

### 2.9 Things to explicitly NOT do in flight

- **Late batch-size ramp.** `grad_accum` is bound at `Trainer` construction and `total_steps`/`warmup_steps` are derived from it there; the WSD schedule is a pure function of `step/total_steps` and checkpoints store `step` only, with no `tokens_seen`. Doubling `grad_accum` on a resume restores `step` in old units while `total_steps` halves: past 25% of the run training silently stops, before 25% it teleports the LR curve to 2x the true progress. That is a schedule-corrupting bug with a silent failure mode, in exchange for about 4% wall clock. If the batch ever does change, it has to change at the same restart where `grad_accum` must be re-divided for 6 or 8 cards, and the step accounting has to be fixed in the same commit.
- **Intra-document attention masking.** Worth about +1 point of downstream average applied for a whole run, so about +0.3 to +0.5 applied to the half you have left. The quoted 4% overhead assumes FA2's varlen path, which sm_75 does not have; on SDPA's mem-efficient backend with an explicit 2048x2048 block-diagonal bias, realistic cost here is 10-30%, which on a 3-10 week run is days to weeks. Skip it for pretraining. Do use it in SFT (see 3.1), where the sequences are short and it is a correctness issue rather than an optimization.

## 3. Post-training upgrades (SFT / DPO)

All data below is free and all of it runs on a 24 GB Turing card.

### 3.1 SFT: replace UltraChat-200k + OASST

**Change:** use **SmolTalk2, no-think subset**, subsampled to roughly 200k to 400k conversations, and keep OpenAssistant in the mix for human-written multi-turn.

**Evidence:** in the one controlled side-by-side at your scale (same base, same hyperparameters, 14 benchmarks, lm-eval-harness), SmolLM2-1.7B base scored 31.13 overall, Tulu-3-SFT-Mix 35.16, SmolTalk 35.49, Orca-AgentInstruct 32.42. There is no published head-to-head against UltraChat+OASST specifically, so this is inferred from the fact that UltraChat and OASST are subsets of both modern mixes. Take the **no-think** half only: a 1.1B model on 100B tokens cannot produce useful long chain-of-thought, and training it to emit reasoning traces costs quality rather than buying it. Drop the long-context and tool-calling subsets, which are wasted at seq 2048. Re-run n-gram decontamination against whatever *you* evaluate on; their decontamination was against their eval set.

**Mechanics that matter:**
- **Block-diagonal packing masks.** If you pack multiple conversations into 2048 tokens without a mask, tokens from one conversation attend to another and separator tokens do not fix it. Threshold Filtering Packing measured up to +7% GSM8K and +4% HumanEval over naive packing. On Turing, build the mask yourself and pass it to SDPA's mem-efficient backend; at seq 2048 a per-sequence bool mask is about 4 MB. The simpler unambiguously-correct alternative is not to pack and to pad instead. **Verify the mask actually applies**: assert that a token in conversation B has exactly zero attention weight on conversation A for one real batch. This is the class of bug that passes every test because nothing asserts its absence.
- **Assistant-only loss** as planned, but consider a small non-zero prompt-loss weight (0.01 to 0.5) if the mix is short-completion heavy; a measured negative quadratic relationship between PLW and score means PLW = 0 is not always optimal there.
- **Replay 5 to 20% pretraining data** into the SFT batches. Measured target-data efficiency gains up to 1.87x, and the paper's own qualifier ("replay helps more when there is less target data present in pre-training") describes your situation exactly. Your FineWeb-Edu shards are already on disk in the loader's format, so this is a mixture ratio and an hour of work. Start at 10%.
- **Use NorMuon, not AdamW.** Optimizer-model consistency is measured and symmetric: matched optimizer beats mismatched in both directions, roughly 5 to 10 points of relative perplexity in the reported trajectories, because Adam and Muon do steepest descent under different norms and a mismatched full fine-tune moves weights against the pretraining geometry. Keep your exact pretraining split (NorMuon on 2D, AdamW on embeddings/1D) at roughly 1/10 the pretraining LR. Fallback if it misbehaves: AdamW + LoRA, which the paper shows is the mitigated path; plain AdamW full fine-tuning is the one to avoid. Caveat: the evidence for the Muon-pretrained direction is thinner than for the Adam-pretrained direction, and one source flags a memorization-over-reasoning tendency for Muon-family optimizers. An SFT run is a day, so run both and compare.

### 3.2 DPO: replace UltraFeedback-binarized, and change the loss and beta

**Change 1, the data:** use **UltraMix-190k** instead of raw UltraFeedback-binarized.

This is the most directly applicable measurement in the whole sweep: OLMo-2-1B-SFT, fixed hyperparameters, length-normalized DPO, 14 benchmarks. SFT baseline 33.29, HelpSteer 34.99, Code-Preference-Pairs 35.30, UltraFeedback 35.68, ORPO-mix 36.56, TuluDPO 37.63, **UltraMix-190k 38.74**. Per-benchmark at 1B, SFT to UltraMix: IFEval 53.49 to 67.13, GSM8K 37.15 to 52.36, HumanEval 19.88 to 31.51, MMLU 40.62 to 42.17. SmolLM2-1.7B-SFT reproduces the ordering with smaller deltas, and notably HelpSteer and Code-Preference-Pairs both made it *worse* than no DPO at all. Do not chase the code subset.

Discount the absolutes: OLMo-2-1B saw about 4T pretraining tokens against your 100B, so trust the ordering, not the numbers.

**Change 2, the loss:** **length-normalized DPO** (divide each sequence's log-ratio by its token count). Tulu 3 reports LN-DPO 47.3 vs vanilla DPO 46.2 vs SFT 42.6, with the gain in math and code. It also removes DPO's documented length-inflation failure, where the model learns "longer equals preferred" and starts rambling, which is very visible in a 1.1B chat demo. Optionally add the SFT NLL loss on the chosen response as an auxiliary regularizer (tune the coefficient; too large collapses DPO back into SFT).

**Change 3, beta:** the planned **0.1 is the Zephyr-7B value, tuned at 7B.** SmolLM2-1.7B, the nearest published analog, used **beta 0.5**, lr 1e-6, 2 epochs on UltraFeedback. Smaller models show a higher degradation rate from DPO, and beta is the direct lever on that. Sweep {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}, evaluate each against the SFT checkpoint on the full held-out suite (not preference win-rate, which is the metric DPO games), and **keep the SFT model as a shippable fallback**. Reference logprobs are precomputed so the sweep is cheap. Measure mean response length before and after; a sharp rise means the objective is being exploited.

**The Turing-specific trap, and it is a real one:** DPO's loss is a difference of log-probability differences at beta 0.1. Computing it under fp16 autocast will lose the signal in rounding noise. **Precompute and store reference logprobs in fp32, and accumulate the DPO loss in fp32.** This is a numerics constraint of the card, not of the recipe.

### 3.3 Optional extras, in order of value per day spent

- **Weight-space soup at the end.** Interpolate the DPO weights back toward SFT (and SFT toward base) and sweep the coefficient in {0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1.0}. This is the standard cheap mitigation for the alignment tax, it is what Llama 3 does, every endpoint is retained, and you pick by measurement. Runs on CPU. If DPO regresses MMLU/ARC, this is how you get most of it back without losing the chat behavior. Pick the coefficient on a held-out slice, not on your reported set.
- **Delta-learning preference pairs.** Generate chosen responses with a mid-size open instruct model and rejected with a clearly smaller one (4-5x parameter gap) on *your own* SFT prompts; the quality gap is the signal and it works even when both generators are weaker than the student. Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on all-weaker-model pairs went MMLU 62.9 to 64.0 and AlpacaEval2 18.7 to 22.4 under DPO, while SFT on the same chosen responses went *down*. This is exactly how SmolLM3's preference set was built (chosen from Qwen3-32B, rejected from Qwen3-0.6B). vLLM supports sm_75 and AWQ int4 is the practical format there, so both generators fit on one spare Titan. Your custom 49,152 BPE is irrelevant here because you consume text, not logits. Budget one card-day for about 100k pairs. Use it as a supplement to UltraMix, not a replacement.
- **Merge candidates as the last step.** Train 2 to 4 SFT/DPO variants and merge in parameter space (model soup, TIES, DARE), pick the best on an internal harness. This is what LFM2 ships as its stage 3. Expect under +1 point. The prerequisite is an eval harness you trust, which you need anyway.

### 3.4 Post-training evaluation protocol

At 1.1B, most of the benchmark movement between two recipes is noise. Use loglikelihood-scored tasks (MMLU, ARC-C, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, TruthfulQA) where next-token probabilities eliminate sampling variance entirely. Use IFEval for instruction following because it is programmatically checked. Compute **question-level paired differences** between two models rather than comparing overall means; that is the single biggest variance reduction available. Average multiple samples per question on generative tasks (pass@1 has been measured to vary by up to 15% across 20 random seeds on small benchmarks).

Avoid MT-Bench and AlpacaEval-style LLM-judge benchmarks entirely: scores shift with judge model, judge prompt and harness version, and TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat scored 3.46 on MT-Bench, which is floor noise. For calibration on expectations: SmolLM2-1.7B after a full high-quality SFT scored HumanEval pass@1 = 1.83. Do not read a near-zero code score as a data problem.

### 3.5 Post-training: argued against

- **GRPO / RLVR on math or code.** RLVR sharpens the base distribution, it does not extend it: RLVR models beat their base at k=1 but the base wins at large k, and the reasoning boundary narrows as training proceeds. The small-scale successes start from bases you do not have (Qwen2.5-0.5B went 33.13% to 63.73% on GSM8K under GRPO, but it was pretrained on about 18T heavily math-filtered tokens and already scored 33% zero-shot). Your base will not have that latent competence to elicit. Cost if you did it: splitting 4-8 Turing cards between a vLLM server and the trainer, roughly halving throughput, for 1 to 2 weeks of implementation, which is the entire budget for SFT + DPO + evaluation. If you want the answer *format*, a few thousand GSM8K chain-of-thought examples in the SFT mix buys it for a day of work.
- **Token-level on-policy distillation.** Blocked by your custom 49,152 BPE not aligning with any open teacher's tokenizer. Also worth naming explicitly as a project-integrity choice: a distilled model's reasoning is largely borrowed, which is a different claim in a capstone than "I trained this." Defensible if stated, but it should be deliberate.
- **SimPO / ORPO / KTO / IPO.** A controlled study of 51 post-training algorithms and a 20-variant DPO taxonomy (5 seeds, 100 runs at 1.5B, plus 0.5B and 7B) found **no DPO variant significantly outperforms vanilla DPO after Bonferroni correction**, and decomposed the variance as model scale about 50pp, training paradigm about 10pp, online vs offline about 9pp, loss function about **1pp**. The widely-quoted SimPO headline is 8B/Gemma on LLM-judged win rates. Do not port it. In Tulu 3's own UltraFeedback ablation SimPO scored 51.8-52.9 against an SFT base of 55.7, i.e. worse than doing nothing.

## 4. Restart-only ideas, and why we are not restarting

**The bar:** a restart must promise a clear multi-percent held-out-loss win, or a large speed win at zero quality cost. Nothing below clears it. The single most useful frame: the best-evidenced architecture change published for 1-3B dense models in 2026 buys about +0.009 on a 12-task average, which is roughly what free post-hoc checkpoint averaging gives you for zero cost.

| Idea | Best measurement | Why not |
|---|---|---|

**Two things already adopted, recalibrated (no action, but get the writeup right).** In the 1.2B controlled replication with a 3-seed noise floor (sigma_base = 0.00208), Gated Attention + QK-Norm scored z = +1.23 (delta +0.003) and Value-Residual z = +0.81 (delta +0.002). Both are *inside* the noise band and both have positive sign, so there is no case for removing them, but there is also no case for claiming a loss win from them. QK-Norm itself is real (z = +2.51, best performer at 3B paired with GeGLU). The defensible claim for the attention gate is **sink-free attention and lower activation kurtosis** (which pays off at quantization time and, per section 2.7, gives you fp16 headroom), not perplexity: Qwen's own Gated Attention paper says "minimal impact on perplexity" after sweeping 30+ variants at 1.7B dense on 3.5T tokens.

Also, a distinction worth having ready: **Sigmoid Attention** (replacing softmax with element-wise sigmoid) is the *worst* method in that table at z = -77.68. That is not the same thing as your per-head sigmoid attention-*output* gate, which appears separately at z = +1.23.

**One standing risk to watch rather than act on.** The HuggingFace Smol Training Playbook reports that Muon and AdeMaMix both achieved lower final loss than AdamW in their small ablations and were rejected for SmolLM3 because they proved prone to divergence when scaled to 3B. Your NorMuon choice came from a 124M A/B that won by 6.9%, which is the same evidence shape. This is not a reason to restart (NorMuon's Moonlight RMS-matched scaling is specifically the fix for Muon's scaling problems, and the run is healthy), but it is a reason to keep the rollback checkpoint cadence tight and to watch grad norms, not only loss.

## 5. Skipped and refuted, one line each

**Verified against the actual code and refuted:**

- **fp16 loss-scale overflow poisoning NorMuon momentum.** Impossible here: `GradScaler._maybe_opt_step` never calls `optimizer.step()` on an overflow step, and `unscale_` + `clip_grad_norm_(1.0)` run first, so Newton-Schulz sees a matrix of Frobenius norm <= 1, four orders of magnitude below the fp16 ceiling. The DeepSpeed bug is a ZeRO architecture bug (momentum fused into the gradient-reduction epilogue), not a Muon bug. Residual: reorder `zeropower_via_newtonschulz5` to normalize in fp32 *before* casting to fp16 (removes an underflow, not an overflow, worth well under 0.1%); ride it along at the next unavoidable restart.
- **Matching NorMuon's validated 1.1B config (beta1 0.9 to 0.95).** Already 0.95. `train/muon.py`'s `momentum` parameter *is* the orthogonalized group's beta1 and defaults to 0.95; `betas=(0.9, 0.95)` is used only inside `_adamw_group` for embeddings and 1D, which is exactly what the paper prescribes for Adam. Applying it would be a literal no-op with a fat-finger risk.
- **Depth-muP LR (3e-4 to 3.4e-4).** Your batch is 0.5M tokens against the paper's 2M, which wants a *lower* LR, and you are at d_model 2048 / 22 layers, off the paper's width/depth ray where the sqrt(16/22) transfer is defined. 3e-4 came from your own 124M A/B. Leave it.
- **AdaGC (per-tensor adaptive clipping).** The paper's own closest cell is +0.14% with Muon on Llama-2 1.3B *in a setting where it reports no loss spikes occurred*, and +0.01% with AdamW. The fp16 rationale is backwards: `unscale_` records `found_inf` before clipping runs, and an overflow happens inside backward where inf/x is still inf, so it would not prevent one skipped step. NorMuon's RMS-0.2 rescale also makes the update gradient-magnitude-invariant for every 2D parameter.
- **More Newton-Schulz steps on final layers.** The 10-step prescription is made at 300B params, a 100x extrapolation from the paper's largest fitted scale of 2.8B; running its own law back to 1.1B puts you further from the failure regime than the largest model they measured. And the ceiling is measured: an exact fp32 SVD polar factor beats 5-step Muon-Jordan by 0.0013 nats on GPT-2 Small, and 4-step *ties* the exact SVD. NorMuon's per-row divide destroys orthogonality by construction one line later anyway.
- **Muon-SW (scale weight decay by eta^2/eta_max).** The paper states the two updates coincide at fixed LR. 168,000 of your 200,000 steps are at eta = eta_max, so it is bit-for-bit identical for 84% of the run; the residual is final matrix weight norms about 6% higher after cautious-WD gating. All experiments are MoE with no dense ablation, and their baseline applies zero decay to embeddings while yours applies 0.1 to the tied matrix. Revisit only if you ever add a nonzero `min_lr_ratio`.
- **HTMuon (singular values to a power p).** The wall-clock-positive variant recovers about a quarter of the headline (0.25 of 0.98 PPL at 135M), the 1B evidence is at ~9-12 tokens/param against your 91, NorMuon already captures much of the same effect, and the fractional matrix root forces fp32, which on Turing runs at ~16 TFLOPS against ~110-130 for the fp16 tensor path. Roughly 3-6x the FLOPs at ~7x lower throughput, per rank, unamortized.
- **Late batch-size ramp.** About 4% wall clock (not 5-15%), delivered via mid-run surgery on step accounting with a silent-corruption failure mode. See 2.9.
- **Checkpoint merging as a decay *substitute*.** WSM is 16.3B MoE on 10.2T tokens with a 25B-token checkpoint interval against your 15B-token entire decay, it replaces decay rather than augmenting it, and it explicitly reports both merge-then-decay and decay-then-merge "failed to yield any improvement." The 1.5B/30B measurement with uniform data ordering is negative (45.29 EMA vs 46.21 WSD).

**Searched and set aside:**

- **Muon^2, MONA, Variance-Adaptive Muon, NAMO, Turbo-Muon, Hierarchical/Cache/Dyn Muon, MuonQ, Muown, Newton-Muon, AMUSE, MuonAll**, all either restart-class update-rule changes with no published 1B head-to-head against NorMuon, MoE-only, or dominated by Gram Newton-Schulz.
- **Gram Newton-Schulz**, mathematically identical, 40-50% off the orthogonalization step, and it *prefers* fp16, which is the one place Turing is an advantage. Not promoted only because the published kernels target Hopper/Blackwell and at `grad_accum` 256 the NS step is already a rounding error. Worth a measurement if throughput ever becomes the binding constraint.
- **8-bit blockwise quantization of the Muon momentum**, 62% optimizer-state reduction at 2.7B with val loss differing by at most 0.002; could buy `micro_batch: 2`. Medium implementation risk, and do not bundle it with any numerics investigation because quantization noise in the momentum buffer looks superficially like the poisoning failure mode.
- **SOAP / KL-SOAP**, 2026 evidence at 8B confirms only "a consistent, albeit slight, edge over Muon". Does not pay for the memory on 24 GB.
- **MuonClip / qk-clip**, redundant, you already have QK-norm.
- **Data-mixing laws (RegMix, Aioli, R&B, Data Mixing Agent)**, all need proxy-model sweeps before the run (RegMix trained 512 1M-param models to predict a 1B mix) or an online reweighting controller; your mix already matches what SmolLM2/3 and OLMo converged on.
- **DiReCT (Hessian-spectrum annealing-sample selection)**, claimed ~2pp at Llama-1.1B, but against a *uniform sampling* baseline, not a hand-picked source mix, and it needs a full gradient-projection pass over the pool plus a convex solve. Section 2.3 captures most of the effect with a config change.
- **Best-fit packing**, real (reading comprehension +4.7%, program synthesis +9.2%, up to 58.3% less closed-domain hallucination) but the scales behind those percentages could not be verified, and it requires document boundary offsets that the flat token stream may not preserve.
- **Ultra-FineWeb-en swap**, +1.33 English average over FineWeb-Edu, but measured by the filter's own authors on their own filter, and only about half the web tokens remain. Fold it into the anneal mix if anywhere.
- **Mid-training context extension to 4096/8192**, zero gain on short-context quality, the SFT/DPO data is overwhelmingly short, and at 8192 the attention matrices are 16x the 2048 case with no FA2. Defer as a separate post-training stage if ever wanted. Never raise the RoPE base without training; that degrades short-context quality.
- **Sequence-length curriculum**, unrealizable without a restart, and changing packed length mid-run silently changes cross-document attention, which is a quality change dressed as a throughput change.
- **OLMo-2-style three-run decay soup**, genuinely measured, but 2 extra 15B-token decay phases is 1 to 3.5 extra weeks on 4 cards for the least-quantified item on the list. Tail-EMA targets the same late-iterate noise for one CPU weight copy.
- **Attention logit soft-capping**, z = +1.18 at 1.2B, inside the noise band. It is a stability tool, not a quality tool, and QK-norm replaces it inside the attention layers.
- **LR re-warmup on the mix change**, does not apply. Re-warmup is for continual pretraining after a *completed* cosine decay; you are in the constant phase, so a mix change needs no LR change at all.
- **Linear-to-zero instead of cosine-to-zero**, the D2Z 60%-compute headline is a win over decaying to 10% of peak. `train/schedule.py` already decays to true zero (`min_lr_ratio` default 0.0, nothing overrides it). Non-issue, recorded so it does not get re-opened.

**Genuine gaps in the literature (nobody has published this):**

- No fp16-specific study of the standard Newton-Schulz iteration at 1B scale. Every Muon precision study is bf16 or fp8. This is a real blind spot for Turing-class hardware.
- No paper tests a mid-run Muon-family optimizer or update-rule swap. Every optimizer gain in this sweep was measured on a from-scratch run.
- No published SFT/DPO recipe for a dense model pretrained on as few as 100B tokens; every 1B-scale post-training result sits on a 2T to 11T base, so trust orderings and not absolutes.
- No clean iso-parameter depth-vs-width ablation at ~1.1B, no controlled GQA-ratio ablation at 1B with a noise floor, and no controlled head-dim 64 vs 128 quality ablation. The field standardized on 128 without measuring it at this scale.
